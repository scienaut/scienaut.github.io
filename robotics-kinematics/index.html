Interactive planar robot analysis with live XY motion, link mass properties, joint friction, two-jaw gripper and payload modelling, and time-history plots for displacement, speed, torque and power.

01 · Robot & analysis

The V1.0 dynamic model is a planar serial revolute arm. Joint angles are entered in degrees.

02 · Link geometry

Link mass means the complete moving-link assembly mass: structure, fasteners, motor, gearbox, cables and sensors. Enter each assembly's total mass once; do not add the same component mass again elsewhere. Centre-of-mass position and inertia are required for dynamic accuracy.

03 · Joint friction

Coulomb friction torque is estimated as μ × normal load × effective friction radius. Viscous coefficient adds a speed-dependent term. Enter measured or manufacturer-supported values.

04 · Gripper & payload

The two-jaw gripper is represented as a lumped tool mass at the final link. Payload is a point mass at the entered tool offset. Jaw opening is visual only in V1.0.

Engineering model boundary: planar rigid links, revolute joints, gravity in the XY plane, quintic joint-space interpolation, lumped link mass and centre of mass. Link rotational inertia is entered or estimated using a uniform slender-rod approximation. Bearing compliance, backlash, gearbox efficiency, motor electrical losses, flexible links, contact/grasp dynamics and collision detection are not included. Torque and power are ideal joint-side mechanical estimates, not motor selection ratings.

Physics behind the motion

01 · Forward kinematics

Each revolute joint rotates its link. The end-effector position is the sum of the link vectors in the XY plane.

x = Σ Lᵢ cos(Σqⱼ)
y = Σ Lᵢ sin(Σqⱼ)

02 · Inverse kinematics

Finds joint angles that place the tool at the requested target. A planar 2R arm can have elbow-up and elbow-down solutions; some targets are unreachable.

cos(q₂) = (x² + y² − L₁² − L₂²)/(2L₁L₂)

03 · Smooth motion profile

The move uses quintic interpolation. Velocity and acceleration are zero at the start and end, avoiding abrupt endpoint changes.

s(u) = 10u³ − 15u⁴ + 6u⁵
q(t) = q₀ + (qf − q₀)s(u)

04 · Link dynamics

Joint torque is obtained from the rigid-body manipulator equation. The mass matrix depends on link masses, centre of mass and rotational inertia.

τ = M(q)q̈ + C(q,q̇) + G(q) + τf

05 · Joint friction

Coulomb friction opposes motion; viscous friction increases with angular speed. The entered coefficients are model inputs, not universal constants.

τf = μNr·sign(q̇) + Bq̇

06 · Speed and power

Tool speed is derived from the end-effector trajectory. Mechanical joint power is torque multiplied by angular velocity; negative power indicates energy returned/braking.

Pᵢ = τᵢq̇ᵢ
v = √(ẋ² + ẏ²)

Interpretation & limits

These are ideal rigid-link joint-side results. Gearbox efficiency, motor electrical losses, compliance, backlash, collision/contact and gripper grasp forces are excluded. Validate inertia and friction against CAD or measurement before selecting hardware.
