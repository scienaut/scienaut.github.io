Machine design · Fastener engineering

Bolt & Fastener Design Calculator

Basic bolt stress screening and detailed elastic bolt-group analysis for direct and eccentric shear, eccentric axial tension and combined loading. SI and FPS display units are supported; calculations use N, mm and MPa internally.

Scope: V1.0 is an engineering screening tool. It is not a complete VDI 2230 joint verification, structural connection code check, fatigue assessment or prying-action analysis. Review all assumptions before design use.

01 · Analysis setup

How to use: Select Basic for a quick screening or Detailed for bolt-group geometry and eccentric loading. Choose SI or FPS before entering values. Set the required factor of safety and identify the joint configuration.

02 · Fastener geometry

How to use: Choose the thread series and nominal diameter. Confirm tensile stress area from the applicable thread standard. Enter the actual shear plane area and number of shear planes based on the physical joint stack-up.

Stress areas are editable. Confirm thread pitch and tensile stress area against the selected ISO metric or Unified thread table; do not use nominal shank area for threaded tension.

03 · Fastener material

How to use: Select the fastener property class only when it matches the specified fastener standard and diameter range. For a special alloy, choose Custom and enter values from a traceable material certificate or manufacturer datasheet.

References: ISO 898-1 for carbon/alloy steel fasteners; ISO 3506 series for stainless fasteners; ASTM F3125 only for products within its scope. Nominal class values shown are screening values. Actual proof stress is diameter/product dependent and must be checked against the applicable standard table. Custom values are user-entered, not certified.

04 · Applied loading

How to use: Enter signed forces and moments at the chosen reference point. Resolve the real applied load into the defined X, Y and axial directions. Avoid entering the same eccentric moment both as a direct moment and as an eccentricity-generated moment.

Mz is about the bolt-group normal and produces in-plane torsional shear. Mx and My create non-uniform axial bolt tension. Signed loads follow a right-handed coordinate convention.

05 · Bolt-group geometry and eccentricity

How to use: Select a bolt pattern and enter centre-to-centre spacing or pitch-circle diameter. Set eccentricity from the bolt-group centroid to the applied load line. For an irregular group, use Custom coordinates and verify every bolt position.

Built-in worked example — eccentric in-plane shear: Four bolts at (±50, ±40) mm; Fx = 4,000 N; eccentricity ey = 100 mm. Thus Mz = −400,000 N·mm and Jg = 16,400 mm². Direct shear = 1,000 N/bolt. Elastic torsional shear components are calculated bolt-by-bolt; maximum resultant = 2,321.69 N. With As = 113.1 mm² and one shear plane, maximum shear stress = 20.53 MPa. Simplified von Mises screening stress = 35.56 MPa; class 8.8 nominal screening proof value 580 MPa gives FOS = 16.31. Use the example button to load and reproduce these values.

Equations used

Centroid: x̄=Σxᵢ/n ; ȳ=Σyᵢ/n
Direct shear: Vx=Fx/n ; Vy=Fy/n
Mz=ex·Fy−ey·Fx+Mz,input
Jg=Σ[(xᵢ−x̄)²+(yᵢ−ȳ)²]
Vmx=−Mz·yᵢ/Jg ; Vmy=Mz·xᵢ/Jg
Vi=√[(Vx+Vmx)²+(Vy+Vmy)²]
σVM=√[(Ft/At)²+3(Vi/As,total)²]

Assumptions and limitations

  • Linear elastic bolt-group method; identical bolt stiffness assumed.
  • Direct shear is shared equally; torsional shear is proportional to radius from group centroid.
  • Axial tension is distributed by rigid-plate linear bolt-spring equilibrium; negative calculated bolt forces are clipped to zero for screening and trigger a contact warning.
  • Shear plane and number of planes are user-selected.
  • No preload, slip, prying, plate bending, tear-out, thread stripping or fatigue certification.

08 · References and limitations

How to use: Check the standards relevant to the selected fastener product and application. Confirm current editions, product scope, material condition and design factors before using results in a released design.

  • ISO 898-1 — Carbon/alloy steel fastener mechanical properties.
  • ISO 3506 series — Stainless steel fastener mechanical properties.
  • ISO 4014 / ISO 4017 — Hexagon bolt product dimensions.
  • ISO 261 / ISO 724 — Metric thread series and basic dimensions.
  • VDI 2230 Part 1 — Highly stressed bolted-joint calculation.
  • NASA RP-1228 — Fastener Design Manual, including fastener groups and combined loading.
  • NASA-STD-5020A — Threaded fastening systems for applicable aerospace use.
  • ASME B18 series and ASTM F3125 — Applicable product dimensions and structural fastener specifications.

This tool does not claim compliance with any listed standard. Stainless fastener tensile classes do not by themselves provide fatigue or shear allowables. For prying-action bracket design, a suitable T-stub or plate/contact model is required; the present diagram is illustrative only.
